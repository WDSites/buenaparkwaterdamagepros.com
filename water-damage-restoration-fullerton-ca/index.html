When water is spreading in Fullerton

Stop the source

Fullerton runs its own water system through the Public Works Water Division, so a leak before your meter is a call to the city. A leak after the meter is yours to stop at the main valve. Turn it off first, then open a faucet at the lowest point in the house to drain the line.

Power only if safe

If water is touching outlets, dripping from a ceiling light or standing near the panel, stay out and leave the breaker alone unless you can reach it on dry floor. In any two story home, a wet ceiling above a dry floor is the common trap, and the light fixture is the danger spot. Say so when you call and the crew will plan around it.

Lift and photograph

Move rugs, paper, electronics and small furniture onto dry surfaces or an upper floor, but photograph everything first. About 52 percent of Fullerton homes are owner-occupied, so a little under half are rented, and renters should send the landlord written notice with those photos the same day.

Keep people and pets out, then call

Water from a sewer line or from rising storm runoff is Category 3, and nobody should wade through it. Clean supply line water can turn Category 2 after a day or two on carpet. Call the 24/7 line from a dry room and describe what is wet.

Local hotspots

Two dams and the winter of 2005

Fullerton sits below two flood control dams. Brea Dam was built in 1942 by the Army Corps of Engineers and controls Brea Creek and its tributaries, and Fullerton Dam, built in 1941, is part of the Santa Ana River mainstem project. If you live or work in north Fullerton, that history is the reason flood questions come up in this city more often than in most of Orange County.

The winter rains of 2005 filled the Brea Dam reservoir and water spilled over the crest, flooding Fullerton Golf Course and parts of Bastanchury Road. No lives were lost. For a property owner the lesson is practical: a heavy storm season can put water in places that look safe in July, so walk your garage, side yards and low doors after the first big winter storm.

The city hazard plan adopted in 2020 points out that dam inundation areas are not necessarily the same as 100-year or 500-year flood areas. We do not state a flood zone for any address, so check the FEMA Flood Map Service Center with your street address. If storm runoff has entered the building, our flood damage restoration service handles the contaminated water, the soaked materials and the drying that follows.

Older downtown buildings and the Skyline Park homes

Downtown Fullerton has a cluster of historic buildings around Downtown Plaza at 125 E Wilshire Ave. Old plaster, hardwood and thick masonry hold moisture longer than modern drywall, so a small pipe leak on an upper floor can leave a wall damp for days after the surface looks dry. A moisture meter reading inside the wall, not a glance at the paint, tells the crew when drying is actually finished.

The Skyline Park Preservation Zone covers 114 lots built in the late 1920s, bounded by Skyline Dr, Cannon Ln, N Lemon St and Sheppard Dr. Homes that age often have original floors and trim that are worth saving, which means controlled drying with air movers and dehumidifiers instead of tearing everything out. Before you remove anything in a preservation area, ask the Building and Safety Division what the city expects.

Downtown ZIP 92832 also includes City Hall and a mix of apartments and businesses. When one unit floods, the unit below usually takes the second hit, so the earlier you call, the fewer rooms end up wet. Our water mitigation work focuses on exactly that, limiting the spread before repairs begin.

City water, city sewer, and who to call first

The Public Works Water Division supplies about 77 percent local groundwater from the Orange County basin and about 23 percent imported Metropolitan Water District water, drawn from 10 wells of which 8 are active. For you, that means the city is your water provider, so a break in the service line before the meter is reported to Public Works rather than to a plumber.

The sewer system is also city-owned, with more than 320 miles of pipe managed by the Public Works Sewer Division. If a toilet, shower and floor drain all back up at once, the blockage is probably in the main line, so call the Sewer Division and then call us. Sewage is Category 3 water, and porous materials it touches usually have to be removed, not dried.

Fullerton Municipal Airport sits on the western edge of the city and is the only general aviation airport in Orange County. Hangars and airfield offices have large roofs and floors, so a roof leak can wet a wide area quickly, and extraction units matter more than small fans. Amerige Heights in western Fullerton is a residential neighborhood where the first move is the same as anywhere, stop the water, photograph it, call early.

Campus and north side

The Fullerton Arboretum at 1900 Associated Rd includes Heritage House, a restored Victorian, and wood-frame buildings of that type need slow, measured drying so floors and trim do not warp. Rental homes near campus are common, so tenants should report a leak to the landlord in writing the moment they see it.

Standing water that came from outdoors is treated as contaminated, so keep out of it and call before you start pumping.

Fullerton: permits, utilities and flood data

Fullerton had a 2020 Census population of 143,617. Repairs that go beyond drying, such as replacing drywall, flooring or plumbing, may need a permit, so ask the Building and Safety Division at 303 W Commonwealth Ave, City Hall second floor, or call (714) 738-6541 before work begins.

Water and sewer questions go to Fullerton Public Works, in its Water Division and Sewer Division. For flood history, read the city hazard plan and check the FEMA Flood Map Service Center for your exact address.

Standard homeowners policies do not cover flood, and flood coverage usually needs a separate policy. The California Department of Insurance notes that a policy may not pay for ensuing damage if you do not take reasonable steps to protect the property after a loss. Ask your insurer what applies to you, or call the department's consumer hotline at 1-800-927-4357.

Which service fits your Fullerton job

Storm runoff, creek overflow and sewage cases call for flood damage cleanup, with extraction, removal of contaminated materials and antimicrobial treatment. A burst pipe in a clean-water line is usually a water mitigation job, where the aim is to stop the spread and start drying quickly. Once the structure is dry, water damage cleanup covers the sanitizing and the removal of what cannot be saved.

Damp drywall and carpet padding can grow mold if they stay wet, and trade guidance puts the critical window at the first 24 to 48 hours. If you already see growth, read about mold remediation. California has no state licensing for mold remediators, and contracted work of $500 or more needs a contractor's license, so ask for the state license number and ask whether crews work to ANSI/IICRC S500 for water and S520 for mold.

FAQs from Fullerton

Do you cover every ZIP code in Fullerton?

Yes. We serve 92831 around Cal State Fullerton and the Arboretum, 92832 in the downtown and City Hall area, 92833 on the southwest and western side near Buena Park, and 92835 in north Fullerton including the Brea Dam Recreation Area. Tell dispatch the ZIP and the nearest cross street when you call.

Is emergency water removal available in Fullerton?

Yes. Emergency dispatch is available around the clock, every day, and a crew can start extraction as soon as the situation is assessed. Stop the source if it is safe, photograph the damage and then call.

Does the 2005 Brea Dam overflow mean my Fullerton home is in a flood zone?

Not necessarily. The city hazard plan says dam inundation areas are not always the same as 100-year or 500-year flood areas. Look up your exact address on the FEMA Flood Map Service Center and ask the city about floodplain questions before you rebuild.

Who do I call if water is leaking at the street in Fullerton?

The city runs the water system through the Public Works Water Division, so a leak before your meter goes to Public Works. Anything inside the meter, such as a broken supply line or water heater, is on your side and our crew can dry the damage.

Do I need a permit for water damage repairs in Fullerton?

Drying often needs none, but rebuilding walls, floors or plumbing might. Contact the Building and Safety Division or visit City Hall at 303 W Commonwealth Ave, second floor, and ask whether your repair needs one.

I rent in Fullerton. What should I do first?

Photograph the damage, stop the water if you can safely do it and notify your landlord in writing. A little under half of Fullerton homes are rented, so this comes up often. Then call us so drying can begin while you and the landlord sort out the repairs.

Does homeowners insurance cover flood water in Fullerton?

Standard homeowners policies generally do not cover flood, and that usually requires a separate policy. Sudden and accidental pipe breaks are often covered, but gradual leaks often are not. Check your policy or ask your insurer, and protect the property from further damage while you wait.
