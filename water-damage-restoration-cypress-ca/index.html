When water is spreading in Cypress

Cypress houses sit on lots laid out under older setback rules, so a leak in one room can reach a neighbouring room or a shared wall faster than you expect. These four steps matter most in the minutes before a crew reaches your door.

Stop the source

Shut the main valve at the front of the house or the fixture shutoff under the sink. Golden State Water supplies Cypress, and the valve on your side of the meter is yours to close, so find it before you need it.

Power only if safe

If water has reached outlets, baseboards or a garage panel, leave the breaker alone and stay out of the wet room. Cut power from a dry spot only, and tell the dispatcher what is wet.

Lift what you can

Move rugs, electronics, books and photo boxes onto dry furniture or upstairs. Wet carpet padding in a Cypress ranch house is usually the first thing to hold moisture, so lift furniture legs off it.

Keep clear and call

Stay out of standing water that came from a drain, toilet or street channel, because it is Category 3 and may carry sewage. Keep children and pets away, then call the 24/7 line.

Storm channels and sewage backups come first in Cypress

The Cypress General Plan Safety Element says the city has no natural permanent water features. Instead, six storm drain channels carry water only occasionally: Moody Creek, Coyote Creek, 2A, Carbon Creek, Stanton Creek and Bolsa Chica Creek. On the 1989 FEMA map, the 100-year flood is contained in the Carbon Creek and Bolsa Chica channels, while the 500-year flood may cover the whole city.

Check the FEMA Flood Map Service Center for your own address, because the citywide picture does not tell you what applies to your lot. The plan says Coyote Creek floodwater stays in its channel, so Cypress is not affected by it. Three dams still matter: Prado, Carbon Canyon and Whittier Narrows. Standard homeowners policies usually exclude flood, so ask your insurer about a separate flood policy before a storm season, not after it.

Rising water from a street channel or a backed-up drain is Category 3 black water. Crews treat it differently from a clean supply-line leak. We pull contaminated porous materials such as carpet, padding and low drywall, run extraction units, and apply antimicrobial treatment before drying starts. Our flood damage restoration work follows that sequence for any water that came from outside the plumbing.

A sewage backup that shows up in several fixtures at once, such as a floor drain, a tub and a toilet, points to the line rather than your own pipe. Call the sewer agency and the city Maintenance Division first, then call for cleanup, so the public side is checked while the affected rooms are sealed off.

The Cypress Trunk Sewer Project and what it means at home

OC San began construction on its Cypress Trunk Sewer Project in summer 2025, and the work runs about three years. The project covers roughly 7 miles of sewer, with work on Denni Street between Lincoln Avenue and Rainier Drive and on Orange Avenue near Alderbury Street. If your home or business is near those streets, expect trenching, traffic changes and some noise for the duration.

Construction does not mean your house will flood, and we do not suggest it does. It does mean that a sewer odor, a slow drain or a gurgling toilet near those streets is worth reporting quickly, because a problem in the main line shows up in the lowest fixtures in a home first. Photograph the fixture, note the time, and tell both OC San and the city Maintenance Division.

If sewage does enter a room, the cleanup is a different job from drying a pipe leak. Crews set containment barriers, use HEPA air scrubbers in affected areas, remove porous materials and treat hard surfaces. Our water damage cleanup crews handle that work, and the earlier the room is isolated, the less of the house is affected.

Older homes, setbacks and mobile home parks

The general plan notes that most Cypress houses were built under the setback rules in effect at the time. Those rules produced the familiar pattern of single-story homes set back from the street with side yards between neighbours. In practice, a leak under a slab or inside a wall cavity often travels along one side of the house, so thermal imaging and moisture mapping matter more than guessing from the visible stain.

Cypress also has two mobile home parks with 373 mobile homes in total. Those homes have thinner floor systems, with subfloor and insulation that stay wet longer than a standard slab. If you live in one, close the water supply at the unit and ask the park management who handles the shutoff, because the lines may be shared.

Because 68.1 percent of Cypress homes are owner-occupied, most water damage here lands on the homeowner's own claim. Check your policy for the difference between sudden and accidental damage, which is often covered, and a gradual leak or neglect, which often is not. Photograph every wet surface before anything is moved, and keep damaged materials until your insurer has seen them.

FAQs from Cypress

Do you cover every ZIP code in Cypress?

Yes. Cypress uses a single ZIP code, 90630, and it covers the whole city, from the Valley View Street corridor and Cypress College to Katella Avenue, Lincoln Avenue and the Civic Center on Orange Avenue. One call to the 24/7 line covers any address inside it.

Is emergency water removal available in Cypress?

Yes. Emergency dispatch is available around the clock, every day, from our Buena Park base. Call, say where the water came from and which rooms are wet, and the dispatcher will tell you what to do while the crew gets ready.

Who do I call for a sewer backup in Cypress?

Call OC San, which runs the regional sewer, and the City of Cypress Maintenance Division for local lines. Then call us for cleanup, because sewage is Category 3 water and needs containment barriers, extraction and antimicrobial treatment.

Does the Cypress Trunk Sewer Project affect my water damage risk?

OC San's roughly 7 mile project, on Denni Street and Orange Avenue near Alderbury Street, began in summer 2025 and runs about three years. It does not mean your home will flood, but report odors, slow drains or gurgling fixtures near those streets to OC San and the city quickly.

Is Cypress in a flood zone?

The city's General Plan says the 100-year flood is contained in the Carbon Creek and Bolsa Chica channels on the 1989 FEMA map, and the 500-year flood may cover the whole city. Check the FEMA Flood Map Service Center for your address, and ask your insurer about flood coverage, since homeowners policies usually exclude it.

Do I need a permit for water damage repairs in Cypress?

Ask the City of Cypress Building Division whether your repair needs a permit before demolition or rebuilding starts. Do not assume small jobs are exempt, and keep photos and records of removed materials.

Will insurance cover water damage in my Cypress home?

Sudden and accidental water damage is often covered, while gradual leaks and neglect often are not, and flood is usually excluded. Check your policy, ask your insurer, and take reasonable steps to protect the property after a loss.
