A slab leak is a leak in a water line that runs through or beneath a concrete foundation. You cannot see the pipe, so the first sign is often indirect, such as a floor that feels warm, a hiss with every tap closed or a water bill that jumped. By the time flooring lifts, water has usually been moving under it for a while.

If water is coming up through the floor or a meter is spinning, treat the first hour as urgent. Our 24/7 line is open, and we can begin drying and extraction while you arrange the plumber. If you are only suspicious, the steps below help you confirm before you tear anything open.

Signs that point to a leak under the floor

Warm or hot floor spot

A hot water line leaking beneath the slab can heat one patch of tile or wood while the rest stays cool.

Running water sound

You hear a faint hiss or rush while every fixture and appliance is off.

Meter keeps moving

The water meter dial turns even though nothing in the house is using water.

Damp baseboards or curling floors

Moisture wicks up wall bottoms, lifts laminate seams, cups hardwood or loosens tile grout.

A musty smell from a closet or hallway, or a bill far above your normal use, adds weight to those signs. None of them proves a slab leak on its own, since a running toilet or a dripping irrigation valve can look similar.

A simple meter check you can do now

  1. Turn off every faucet, appliance and irrigation timer, and make sure no one flushes a toilet.
  2. Find the water meter, usually in a box near the curb or the edge of your lot, and lift the lid.
  3. Look at the small dial or triangle that spins with any flow. Note its position or take a photo.
  4. Wait 15 to 30 minutes with everything still off and look again.
  5. If the dial moved, water is escaping somewhere. Then close the main shutoff valve and see whether it stops.

If it keeps moving after the main is closed, the leak is probably on the street side. If it stops, the leak is on your side, and a leak detection plumber can find the line. We do not locate pipes ourselves, and no home visit can promise to detect every leak.

Safety steps in the first hour

Close the main

Turn the main shutoff on your property clockwise until it stops. This limits the water that keeps feeding the slab.

Mind the electricity

If water is pooling near outlets, appliances or the panel, do not step into it. Shut off power to that area only if you can reach the breaker on dry ground.

Lift what you can

Move rugs, boxes and furniture legs off wet floors. Put foil or plastic under heavy pieces that must stay.

Call both trades

Call a plumber for the pipe and us for the water. Photograph wet floors, baseboards and the meter before anything is moved.

Do not drill or chip into the slab yourself, and do not run the dishwasher or washer to test the line. Both can widen the problem.

How we dry floors and walls after a slab leak

Water that rises through a slab spreads sideways under flooring faster than people expect. We use moisture meters and thermal imaging to map how far it has gone, then extract standing water with extraction units. Carpet and pad are lifted, and wet baseboards are pulled so the bottom of the wall can breathe.

Tile and hardwood need a different approach than carpet. Floor-drying systems with mats and air movers pull moisture out from beneath the surface, while LGR dehumidifiers lower the humidity of the whole room. Ask whether a company's crews work to ANSI/IICRC S500, the published industry reference, and how they confirm the slab and walls are dry before you rebuild.

If drywall has wicked water up a foot or more, our drywall water damage repair page covers cutting and replacing it. Long runs of wet flooring are explained on our structural drying page.

What changes the cost

  • The type of flooring over the leak, since tile, hardwood, laminate and carpet dry in different ways.
  • How far the water traveled under the floor and up the walls.
  • Whether the leak was caught at once or ran for weeks.
  • How many rooms need equipment and how many days the readings take to drop.
  • What must be removed to reach the pipe and what the plumber's access requires.
  • Whether growth, smell or contents require added treatment or containment.

Insurance and slab leaks

The California Department of Insurance explains that coverage turns on cause and timing. Sudden and accidental discharge is often covered, while long-term seepage, corrosion or neglect is often excluded. Some policies treat the cost of reaching the pipe differently from the cost of the water damage itself.

The department also says policies may not pay for further damage if you do not take reasonable steps to protect the property. Shutting off the main, drying promptly and keeping receipts count as those steps. Read your policy, ask your insurer about your situation and call the department's consumer line at 1-800-927-4357 if you have questions about the process.

Buena Park: city water, the meter and permits

The City of Buena Park runs its own water utility, with about 19,000 service connections and roughly 225 miles of pipeline. The main shutoff is on your property, and a leak between the street and the meter is the city's to handle. If the meter keeps moving after your main is closed, call the city water utility for the street side.

Ask the Buena Park Building Division whether floor, wall or plumbing work in your home needs a permit. Cutting a slab or opening walls to reach a pipe may trigger rules we cannot predict for your address, and a plumber will usually know what to ask.

California requires a state contractor's license for construction or improvement work of 500 dollars or more, according to the Contractors State License Board. Ask any contractor rebuilding your floor for their license number and check it before work starts.

Slab and raised foundation homes

If you can see a crawl space vent or an access hatch outside, your home may be raised, and our crawl space water damage page is the better read.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Ignoring a high water bill because the house looks dry.
  • Laying new flooring over a floor that has not been dried and tested.
  • Leaving the main on while you wait for the plumber.
  • Assuming hot water use is the cause of a warm floor without checking the meter.
  • Waiting for a stain to appear before calling. Hidden water can run for weeks.

Common questions

Is emergency slab leak water damage cleanup available in Buena Park?

Yes. Our line at (714) 515-1308 is open 24/7. We can extract standing water, lift wet carpet and begin drying while a plumber finds and repairs the pipe. Close the main shutoff first so the leak does not keep feeding the floor.

How do I tell if my meter shows a leak?

Turn off all water use, open the meter box and note the position of the small flow dial. Wait 15 to 30 minutes and check again. If it moved, water is escaping. Close the main and look again to tell whether the leak is on your side or the street side.

Who fixes the pipe, you or a plumber?

A plumber or leak detection company finds and repairs or reroutes the line. Our work starts after the water source is controlled, or alongside it when floors are already wet. We dry the floors and walls and handle the damage left behind.

Whose responsibility is a leak between the street and my meter?

In Buena Park, the city water utility handles the street side of the meter. The main shutoff on your property marks the start of your responsibility. If the meter dial moves with your main closed, call the city utility.

Do I need a permit for floor and wall repairs?

We cannot tell you for your address. Ask the Buena Park Building Division whether the work you plan needs a permit, and check with your plumber. Ask the contractor for a California license number for work of 500 dollars or more.

Will my homeowners policy pay for slab leak damage?

It depends on the cause and the policy. Sudden and accidental water damage is often covered, while slow seepage is often excluded. Read your policy and ask your insurer, or contact the California Department of Insurance consumer line.
