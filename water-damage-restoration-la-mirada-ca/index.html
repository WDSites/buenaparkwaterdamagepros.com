When water is spreading in La Mirada

Stop the source first

Find the main shutoff valve on your own property and close it. Most La Mirada homes are served by Suburban Water Systems, and the valve at your house is yours to turn. If the leak is on the street side of the meter, call your water provider instead.

Power off only if it is safe

If water is near outlets or the panel and you can reach the breaker without stepping through water, switch off that circuit. If the panel itself is wet, stay out and tell us on the call. Older tract homes can have aging wiring, so do not guess.

Lift what you can

Move rugs, electronics, books and small furniture to dry ground. Pull photos and papers off low shelves. In a single-story house the water reaches every room fast, so work from the lowest and wettest area outward.

Keep people and pets out, then call

Water from a washing machine, a toilet line or a backed-up drain can carry contamination. Keep kids and pets out of it and call the 24/7 line. Tell us when the water started and which rooms are wet so the right equipment is loaded.

Local hotspots

Older storm drains, La Mirada Creek and the heavy-use buildings around La Mirada Blvd see the most unusual water problems. Pool decks, ball fields and theater spaces each bring their own drying needs.

1950s tract homes and what they hide

The city's own Housing Element puts the median year built in La Mirada at 1960. About 80 percent of homes went up before 1980, and 49.4 percent were built in the 1950s alone. Another 14.8 percent date to the 1960s and 12.9 percent to the 1970s. If you live here, ask a plumber what your supply and drain lines are made of before a small leak turns into a big one.

Many houses from that era are single-story. When a pipe fails inside a wall, water spreads sideways through drywall, baseboards and flooring rather than dropping to a lower floor. Moisture meters and thermal imaging cameras let us trace that path before we open anything, which keeps demolition limited to what is actually wet.

Aging drain lines are another common cause. A slow stoppage in a kitchen or bathroom line can seep behind cabinets for weeks before you smell it. If a stain grows or a floor feels soft near a tub, shut off the fixture and call. Wet drywall and carpet padding can grow mold if they are not dried within the first 24 to 48 hours, which is general trade guidance and not a promise about any single job.

With 76.1 percent of La Mirada homes owner-occupied per the 2020 Census, most of the people calling us own the house and decide the repair scope themselves. If you are one of the renters in the other 24 percent or so, photograph everything and tell your landlord in writing the same day. That written record matters when responsibility for the repair is discussed.

Our water damage repair crews handle the rebuild side once the structure is dry, and the drying itself falls under water mitigation. Keeping those two stages straight helps you read an estimate and compare companies fairly.

A contract city: who handles permits

La Mirada is a contract city, which means the city does not run its own building department. Building and Safety is handled by the County of Los Angeles Public Works, Building and Safety Division. Planning sits at City Hall, 13700 La Mirada Blvd. That split surprises many homeowners who assume one counter at City Hall handles everything.

We cannot tell you whether your repair needs a permit, because it depends on the scope. Ask the city or LA County Public Works Building and Safety before you close up walls or replace a water heater. A quick call first costs you nothing and can save you from tearing out finished work later.

This matters most after a bigger loss. If you are replacing drywall across several rooms, rebuilding a bathroom or moving plumbing, ask the permit question before the contractor schedules. A reliable company will not mind waiting for a straight answer from the right office.

Remember too that this is a Los Angeles County city, not an Orange County one. Our dispatch base is in Buena Park, but the agencies you deal with here are the Los Angeles County ones.

La Mirada Creek and the flood overlay

La Mirada Creek flows through the city as a flood control channel and crosses the Biola University area. Coyote Creek crosses I-5 at La Mirada, and the FEMA map shows that crossing as Zone X, the 0.2 percent annual chance flood. The city code, Chapter 21.32, creates a Flood Hazard Overlay zone over FEMA special flood hazard areas. If you are buying, building or remodeling, ask the city whether your lot touches that overlay.

A federal funding release from the mid 2000s described the city's storm drain system as more than 50 years old at that time and the city as prone to localized flooding. We do not cite a flood date, and we will not tell you which zone your address sits in. Check the FEMA Flood Map Service Center with your street address, then ask the city to confirm.

Insurance is the practical side of flood risk. Sudden and accidental water damage, such as a burst supply line, is often covered by a homeowners policy, while gradual leaks and neglect often are not. Flood is usually excluded and needs separate flood coverage. Check your policy, or ask your insurer, before you assume anything.

If floodwater or sewage is involved, treat it as Category 3 black water. Our water damage cleanup crews extract it, remove porous materials that cannot be saved and apply antimicrobial treatment, while you stay out of the affected rooms.

La Mirada: utilities, permits and flood data

Water: most of the city is served by Suburban Water Systems, through the La Mirada Water System. Ask them about the street side of your meter, and treat everything after the meter as yours. Sewer: the Los Angeles County Sanitation Districts. If several fixtures back up at once, call them and a plumber, then call us for the cleanup.

Permits: La Mirada is a contract city, with Building and Safety run by LA County Public Works and Planning at City Hall, 13700 La Mirada Blvd. Ask the city or LA County Public Works Building and Safety whether your repair needs a permit. Flood: check the FEMA Flood Map Service Center for your address and ask the city about the Chapter 21.32 Flood Hazard Overlay.

Mold: California has no state licensing of mold remediators, according to the California Department of Public Health. Contracted work of $500 or more needs a state contractor's license, so ask any company for its California contractor license and whether its crews follow ANSI/IICRC S520. For water jobs, ask the same about ANSI/IICRC S500.

Who handles sewer backups in La Mirada?
