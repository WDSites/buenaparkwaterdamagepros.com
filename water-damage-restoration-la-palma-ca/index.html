Winter storms and dry-season leaks

The National Weather Service 1991 to 2020 normal for Downtown Los Angeles is 14.25 inches of rain a year, with February the wettest month and August the driest. That is the regional picture, not a La Palma station figure. It describes a dry climate where a few winter storms deliver most of the rain, so a roof that sat untouched all summer is suddenly tested in a single week.

Before the first big storm, look at roof flashing, gutters, window seals and the stucco around doors. After a storm, check ceilings for brown rings and walls for soft paint, because slow seepage hides until drywall is already saturated.

The dry months are not leak-free. Supply lines, water heaters, washing machine hoses, irrigation lines and air conditioner drain pans fail in August as readily as in February. If you find a wet floor in summer, shut the valve, then call, since wet carpet padding and drywall can start growing mold if they are not dried within the first 24 to 48 hours.

Who do I call about a leak between the street and my meter in La Palma?

The City of La Palma runs its own water utility, so the street side of the meter is the city's responsibility. Everything after the meter is yours. Shut your main valve if water is entering the home, then call the city and us.

Does a La Palma repair need a permit?

Ask the Building and Safety Division at City Hall, 7822 Walker St before you rebuild. Permit rules vary by the type of repair, and we do not want you to guess. A short call before the walls close can save a lot of trouble.

How soon should drying start after a winter storm leak?

As soon as you can stop the source. Wet drywall and carpet padding can grow mold if they are not dried within the first 24 to 48 hours, which is general trade guidance and not a promise. Regional rain is concentrated in winter, so a storm week can produce several leaks at once.

Will my homeowners policy cover water damage in La Palma?

Sudden and accidental water damage is often covered, while gradual leaks and neglect often are not. Flood is usually excluded and needs separate coverage. Check your policy, ask your insurer, and photograph everything before cleanup starts.

My La Palma home was built in the 1960s. Should I worry about hidden leaks?

Older supply lines and fittings do fail, and damage can sit behind drywall for weeks. Watch for musty smells, bubbling paint and warped baseboards. A moisture meter and thermal imaging camera can tell us what is wet without opening every wall.
