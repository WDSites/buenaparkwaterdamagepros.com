Old Buena Park is the part of the city where most of its historic buildings stand, west of Beach Boulevard around Ninth, Tenth and Eleventh Streets. If a leak, a supply line failure or a sewer backup has hit an older home here, this page explains what is different about drying a house of that age and what to do first.

We serve Old Buena Park as part of our Buena Park coverage. The facts below come from the city's own planning documents and public records.

What the city documents say about Old Buena Park

The city General Plan environmental report describes a Historic Overlay as a small area west of Beach Boulevard between Ninth and Eleventh Streets, and says most of the city's historic resources are inside it. A related city policy covers the area generally bounded by Eleventh Street, Western Avenue, Ninth Street and Beach Boulevard.

The same report says many historic structures are in the Whitaker Addition, which lies between Western and Knott Avenues. It also calls for an "Old Buena Park Historic District" through an overlay or specific plan. The overlay is real, but the district name describes a goal, so we call the area Old Buena Park and give no exact boundary.

Landmarks that anchor the area

Named buildings in city records include the Whitaker-Jaynes House, moved to 6631 Beach Boulevard in 1994, the William E. Tice House at 6591 Beach Boulevard, built in 1905, and the Stage Stop Hotel at 6603 Beach Boulevard, from the 1870s. City Hall is at 6650 Beach Boulevard. Homes listed as historic include the Hasson Home at 7611 10th Street and the Trapp House at 8352 Crescent Avenue.

Two of them, 6631 Beach Boulevard and 7611 10th Street, sit in ZIP 90621. They are landmarks we use to place the area, and they are not our customers. A private home on these streets is treated like any other home when you call.

Older homes are the exception in Buena Park

The city housing element counts about 23,700 housing units from the 2015 to 2019 survey. Only about 1 percent were built in 1939 or earlier, and about 3.5 percent date from the 1940s. The largest share, close to 39 percent, was built in the 1950s. So a house in Old Buena Park that predates the postwar boom is uncommon.

Age alone does not tell us a home has had more water damage, and we do not claim it. What age changes is the materials the crew may meet, which is why the next section matters.

What drying an older home involves

Older houses can have plaster walls, solid wood floors, original trim and older finishes. Plaster and solid wood respond differently to water than modern drywall and engineered flooring, and some trim cannot be replaced like for like. Tell the dispatcher what the walls and floors are made of, if you know.

If paint, flooring or insulation in an older home looks old and may need to be removed, ask whether it should be tested first. Suspected hazardous materials should not be sanded, scraped or broken up by the homeowner. Say so on the call so the crew can plan. The ANSI/IICRC S500 standard is the industry reference for water damage work, so ask any company which standard its crews follow.

First steps when water is spreading in an older home

Find and stop the source

Shut the main water valve if a supply pipe has failed. Older homes may have valves that are stiff or hard to reach, so do not force one that will not turn. Tell the dispatcher if you cannot close it.

Stay out of wet electrical areas

Do not use outlets, lamps or appliances in a wet room. Do not switch breakers while standing in water. Leave the panel alone if you cannot reach it dry.

Protect what cannot be replaced

Move photographs, papers and small keepsakes to a dry place where that is safe. Leave original floors, trim and built-in features in place, and photograph them before anything is moved.

Photograph and call

Photograph the source and the wet areas first. Then phone (714) 515-1308 with your street address, the age of the house and what the soaked walls and floors are made of.

Historic overlay: ask before you rebuild

If your property lies inside the city's Historic Overlay, ask the Buena Park Planning Division whether any repair needs review before work begins. We do not know which individual properties are covered, and we cannot advise on historic rules. Drying and extraction can usually begin on an urgent basis, and decisions about replacing historic features can wait for that answer.

Insurance and documentation

Whether a policy pays depends on what caused the water and what your contract says, and only your insurer can answer that. A record of photos, meter readings and the planned work can describe the loss, though it never promises a payout. Flood is typically excluded from ordinary California homeowners policies. Owners of older houses should also read how their policy treats original materials, because some policies limit like-for-like replacement.

Old Buena Park: utilities, flood data and permits

Water here comes from the city's own utility. Anything between the street main and your meter belongs to the city, while pipes past the meter are the owner's. OC San manages the regional sewers, and its Buena Park Sewer Improvements Project lists sections of Beach Boulevard and Knott Avenue, with construction planned for 2027 to 2028.

Neighborhood-specific FEMA flood-zone status not verified for Old Buena Park. The wider city is mostly in the 500-year zone on FEMA maps, but that says little about one lot, so look your address up on the FEMA Flood Map Service Center. Permit questions go to the Buena Park Building Division.

Old Buena Park questions

Do you serve Old Buena Park?

We take calls from the historic streets west of Beach Boulevard and the homes around them. Read the dispatcher your address and they will confirm the property is covered.

Are older homes more likely to have water damage?

We have no evidence that they are. What age changes is the materials involved, such as plaster, solid wood floors and older finishes, which can need different handling when wet.

Do I need permission to repair a historic home after water damage?

It depends on whether the property is covered by the city's Historic Overlay and what the repair involves. Ask the Buena Park Planning Division before replacing original features. Emergency drying can usually start first.

Can plaster walls be dried after a leak?

Sometimes, depending on how long they stayed wet and how far the water traveled. Moisture readings decide it. We do not promise that every wall or floor can be saved.

Should I test old paint or flooring before removal?

If a material is old and may contain something hazardous, ask a qualified professional about testing before it is disturbed. Do not scrape, sand or break it up yourself.

Is Old Buena Park in a flood zone?

We could not confirm FEMA status for the area as a whole. Flood maps are read lot by lot, so enter your address on the FEMA Flood Map Service Center and ask the city if the result is unclear.
