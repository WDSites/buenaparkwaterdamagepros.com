When water is spreading in Anaheim

A broken supply line, a failed water heater or a backed-up drain does not wait for business hours, and in a city this size the first minutes decide how far the water travels. These four steps are the ones that matter most before a crew reaches your door.

Stop the source

Shut the main valve at your property if you can reach it, or the fixture valve under the sink or behind the toilet. In a rented Anaheim apartment, call the manager or landlord at the same time, because more than half of the city's homes are rented and the building shutoff may not be in your unit.

Power only if it is safe

If water is near outlets, a ceiling light fixture or a panel, leave the breaker alone unless you can reach it while standing on dry floor. Never walk through standing water to get to the electrical box. Tell the dispatcher what is wet and we will talk you through it.

Lift what you can

Move rugs, shoes, laptops, photos and paperwork onto a dry counter or a high shelf. In an older Anaheim Colony bungalow with hardwood floors, pull furniture legs out of the wet area and slide foil or plastic under them so stain does not wick up.

Keep people and pets out, then call

Water from a toilet, a sewer line or outside the building can carry bacteria, so keep children and animals away from it. Call the 24/7 line at (714) 515-1308 and tell us your ZIP, whether the water is clean or dirty, and which floor it is on.

Resort and stadium districts: big buildings, many rooms

The Anaheim Resort covers about 1,100 acres and includes Disneyland Resort and Anaheim GardenWalk, with hotels and restaurants packed around them. A burst riser or a kitchen supply line in a multi-story hotel can push water through ceilings into rooms on two or three floors below. If you manage a property there, shut off the zone valve first, then call so extraction units and air movers can be sized by floor instead of by room.

The Platinum Triangle surrounds Angel Stadium and the Honda Center, with The Grove of Anaheim nearby, and these areas are reached by I-5, SR-57 and SR-22. Mixed-use buildings here often stack apartments over retail, so a leak in one unit can wet a shop below it. Ask your building engineer for the unit shutoff location now, and write it on the inside of your closet door.

Historic colony homes: old floors, old walls, slow drying

The Anaheim Colony Historic District began as an 1857 German colony and covers 1.8 square miles. Most of its roughly 1,100 contributing homes were built between 1910 and 1935, in Craftsman bungalow, Spanish Colonial and other styles. Homes this old often have solid wood floors, plaster or thick lath walls and raised foundations, and all of them dry more slowly than a modern slab-built tract house.

That is why we use moisture meters and thermal imaging cameras to map wet framing before anything is torn out, and why we run LGR dehumidifiers with air movers rather than guessing. Pulling more plaster than needed costs you in repair time, and leaving a wet wall cavity behind invites mold. If you own a home in the district, ask your repair contractor how historic finishes will be protected before demolition starts.

Anaheim Hills: slopes, drains and east side water

Anaheim Hills covers the east side of the city in ZIPs 92807 and 92808, and includes the Anaheim Hills Golf Course. Homes on slopes shed water downhill, so a leak on an upper floor or a blocked gutter can send water into a lower level or garage quickly. Check where downspouts discharge and keep them pointed away from the foundation.

Areas east and south of the 57/91 interchange mostly receive imported Metropolitan Water District water, while central and western Anaheim get groundwater or a mix of the two. That mix does not change how we dry a building, but it is worth knowing when you ask the city about a pressure problem. Call Anaheim Public Utilities for anything on the street side of your meter.

Utilities, sewers and flood data in Anaheim

Anaheim Public Utilities Water Services has supplied the city since 1879, drawing on Orange County groundwater basin wells and imported Metropolitan Water District water. Sewer and storm drain maintenance sits with the city's Operations Division. If sewage rises from several drains at once, stop using water in the home and call the city, then call us for the cleanup, because sewage is treated as Category 3 black water.

The city's draft Safety Element, a public review draft from July 2022, says FEMA places the vast majority of Anaheim in the 500-year flood zone (Zone X). Portions of the Santa Ana River channel and Carbon Creek are in the floodway, and Carbon Creek Channel is owned by the OC Flood Control District. Check the FEMA Flood Map Service Center for your exact address, because a zone for the whole city says nothing about your lot.

The same draft names Prado Dam in Riverside County as the greatest dam failure risk, with floodwater reaching Anaheim in about 6.5 hours at roughly 4 ft depth. Carbon Canyon Dam in Brea could flood areas north of SR-91, according to the general plan EIR. Standard homeowners policies usually exclude flood, so ask your insurer whether you need a separate flood policy.
