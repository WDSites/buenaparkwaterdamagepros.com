Downtown Buena Park is the area around the Buena Park Downtown mall on La Palma Avenue, near Knott's Berry Farm. It mixes retail, apartments and older homes, and a large housing project is planned on the former Sears site. If water has entered a shop, an apartment or a house here, this page explains what to do first and how restoration works.

We serve Downtown Buena Park as part of our Buena Park coverage. The details below come from public records and news reports, and we flag anything that may have changed.

What Downtown Buena Park is

The Buena Park Downtown mall is an enclosed shopping center at 8308 On the Mall, in ZIP 90620. News reports place the former Sears site next to the mall at La Palma and Stanton Avenues. Knott's Berry Farm is a few blocks away.

Because the mall and the surrounding streets mix stores, apartments and houses, a water loss here can involve a business, a multi-unit building or a single home. We do not claim official boundaries for the district.

A large housing project on the former Sears site

A 2023 business report described a plan from Merlone Geier Partners for 1,302 homes on the 25-acre former Sears site. The plan called for 1,176 apartments in four buildings of five to seven stories, and 126 three-story townhomes, with construction in phases over several years.

Later reports say demolition of the former Sears began in 2026. We cannot confirm the current schedule, so ask the city for the latest status. For restoration, the point is simple. More multi-story housing nearby means more buildings where one leak can reach several floors.

Apartments and multi-unit buildings

City estimates put about a third of Buena Park housing units in multifamily buildings, and about 45 percent of households rent. In an apartment, report the leak to your property manager in writing right away, and keep a copy. Photograph the damage and any belongings that were wet.

Water from a unit above can travel through the ceiling, walls and floors below. Several households may need drying at once, so the building manager and the crew should coordinate. Ask your own insurer about renters coverage for belongings.

Shops, restaurants and other businesses

A leaking roof drain, a failed fire sprinkler line or a burst restroom supply can soak flooring and inventory in a retail space. Tell your landlord and your insurer promptly, and photograph the damage before anything is moved.

Our commercial water damage page explains how a crew dries a business and what to ask about downtime. We do not promise how fast any particular business can reopen, because that depends on the damage.

First steps when water is spreading

Stop the source if it is safe

In a house or apartment, close the shutoff valve for the fixture or the unit. In a larger building, call the property manager so the building shutoff can be used. Do not enter standing water near electrical equipment.

Avoid electrical hazards

Do not touch outlets, panels or appliances that are wet or sitting in water. Do not operate breakers while standing in water. If a ceiling is sagging with water, stay out of the room.

Protect people and property

Keep people and pets away from contaminated water. Move small items to dry places where it is safe, and leave heavy fixtures alone until the area is assessed.

Document and call

Record the leak and the wet areas on your phone, then dial (714) 515-1308. Share the exact address or unit number, whether neighbors are affected and what you can see.

How restoration works in a multi-unit or commercial space

A crew finds the source, checks for safety hazards and removes standing water. Moisture readings map how far the water traveled into floors, walls and ceilings, and drying equipment is placed to match. In a multi-unit building, moisture can sit in adjoining units that look dry, so readings in neighboring spaces matter.

Materials that stay saturated, such as ceiling tile, insulation and padding, may need to be removed. We explain what can be dried and what cannot after the readings, and we do not promise that everything can be saved. The ANSI/IICRC S500 standard is the industry reference for water damage work, so ask any company which standard its crews follow.

Insurance and documentation

Tenants should ask their own insurer what a renters policy covers, and business owners should ask the landlord what the building policy includes. The cause of the water decides most coverage questions. Pictures and drying records document what happened but cannot promise payment, and flood damage normally needs its own policy.

Downtown Buena Park: utilities, flood data and permits

The city operates the water system, so a break on the street side of the meter goes to the city. OC San runs the regional sewers. If several drains back up together, treat the water as sewage and keep people out until it is handled.

Neighborhood-specific FEMA flood-zone status not verified for Downtown Buena Park. Flood maps apply to individual properties, so search your address on the FEMA Flood Map Service Center. For tenant improvements or repairs after a loss, the Buena Park Building Division can say if a permit applies.

Services most Downtown calls need

Jobs downtown tend to start with emergency water removal, then structural drying and, for ceilings, drywall water damage repair. Stormwater and roof failures are covered under storm water damage.

Downtown Buena Park questions

Do you serve Downtown Buena Park?

We take calls from the streets around the mall and La Palma Avenue, including apartments and shops. Tell the dispatcher the address and unit number and they will confirm coverage.

What should an apartment tenant do after a leak?

Tell the property manager in writing and keep a copy. Photograph the damage and any wet belongings, and ask your insurer about renters coverage. If a ceiling is sagging, stay out of the room.

Can a leak in one apartment damage the units below?

Yes. Water can travel through ceilings, walls and floors into other units. That is why the building manager and the restoration crew should coordinate, and why neighboring spaces may need moisture readings.

Do you work on businesses as well as homes?

Yes, we take calls for businesses, including shops and restaurants. Tell the dispatcher about the type of space and whether the building has a landlord or a property manager.

Is the former Sears site project built yet?

We cannot confirm its current status. A 2023 report described a plan for 1,302 homes, and later reports say demolition began in 2026. Ask the City of Buena Park for the latest information.

Is Downtown Buena Park in a flood zone?

We could not confirm FEMA status for the district as a whole. Maps are read property by property, so look up your building on the FEMA Flood Map Service Center or ask your landlord.
